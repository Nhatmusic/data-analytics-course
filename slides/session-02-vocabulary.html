🎯 Why three steps today, not one

Most classes stop at "here's how you'd clean data." We're doing steps 1 through 3 back to back so you feel how they depend on each other: a badly defined problem makes it impossible to know what "clean" even means, and cleaning without a question just tidies data for its own sake.

Before anyone opens a spreadsheet, we say out loud what we're actually trying to find out. The first step is simply to check whether we can trust this data at all.

The scenario

A small cafe's point-of-sale system exported its transaction history — 10,000 rows of synthetic sales data. The owner wants two things: total sales for each item, and total sales for each month. But those numbers only mean something if the data behind them is trustworthy — so before we calculate anything, we check: can we actually trust this dataset?

Step 1 — turn placeholders into real blanks
Find & Replace (Ctrl+H), whole sheet: replace ERROR with nothing, then UNKNOWN with nothing. This doesn't fix anything yet — it just makes the fake values visible to COUNTBLANK and filters.
Step 2 — recompute what's derived (from Level 4: TXN_4271903)
Total Spent isn't measured, it's calculated — Quantity × Price Per Unit. Recompute it instead of guessing:
=C2*D2
Your turn Drop that formula down the whole Total Spent column.
Step 3 — decide, for what can't be recomputed
One question decides it: will this column get added up, or grouped into buckets?
  • Goes into math (a SUM or AVERAGE, like Price or Total Spent) — leave it blank. Blank cells get skipped by those formulas automatically — a "Missing" text label would break the math instead.
  • Goes into a chart or group-by (a category like Payment Method or Location) — label it "Missing." A blank cell here just disappears from the chart, making it look like every row had a clean answer. A "Missing" label shows up as its own bar, so the gap stays visible.
  • Impute the mode (fill with the most common value) — standard in real pipelines, but flag it out loud if you do it. It quietly makes the data look more complete than it is.
Example: Payment Method has Cash, Card, (blank), Cash, (blank), Card. Leave the blanks and a chart shows only Cash and Card — looks clean, but hides that 2 of 6 rows had no answer. Label them "Missing" and the chart shows Cash, Card, and Missing — now the gap is honest, not hidden.
To apply "Missing" in Sheets: filter the column to blank cells, type Missing into the first one, then fill it down through the filtered rows.
Your turn For Payment Method and Location, decide blank vs. "Missing" using the question above — then compare to what you brainstormed. Same call or different? Either is fine, defend it.
what NOT to do →
Don't delete a whole row over one placeholder cell. TXN_4271903's Total Spent is a one-formula fix — deleting the row throws away Item, Quantity, Payment Method, and Date to dodge a two-second recalculation.

Quick recall

This dataset: 10,000 transactions, 8 columns. COUNTIF finds bad values like "ERROR" and "UNKNOWN." COUNTA vs. COUNTUNIQUE catches duplicate Transaction IDs. A derived column (Total Spent) gets recomputed, not guessed. For everything else: blank if it feeds a SUM or AVERAGE, "Missing" if it feeds a chart or group-by — never an invented value.
