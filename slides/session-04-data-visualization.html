Right chart type, wrong choice ⚠️

Level 4 taught you what each chart type is built for. Here are two cases where someone picked a technically valid chart type — and it still didn't work.

Case 1 — Too many pie slices
🎯 The question

What share of job postings comes from each industry sector?

⚠️ 9 sectors, one pie
Technically the right chart type — it is "part of a whole." Nine slices in, you can't tell which is bigger without reading every label.
✅ Fixed: bar, sorted
Same data. Sort a bar chart top to bottom and the biggest sector is obvious in one glance — no legend-matching required.
The rule this proves

Pie charts work at 3–4 slices. Past that, group the small ones into "Other," or just switch to a bar chart. Picking a valid chart type isn't the same as picking the right one for how much data you actually have.

Case 2 — Bar chart for a stock price trend
🎯 The question

Is this stock's closing price trending up or down over the last 15 trading days?

⚠️ Bar chart, 15 trading days
Technically the right chart type — bars do compare values. But each day sits in its own separate block, so the trend gets chopped up instead of connected.
✅ Fixed: line chart
Same 15 days. A line connects each day to the next, so the climb — and any dip — is visible in one glance instead of bar-by-bar comparison.
The rule this proves

Bar charts compare separate categories. Once your x-axis is time — days, months, years — use a line chart instead. It shows the connection between one point and the next that bars can't.
