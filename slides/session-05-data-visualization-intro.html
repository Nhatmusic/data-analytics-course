Quick question

Just from that table: which country made the most revenue, and roughly how much more than the smallest one? Don't scroll back and forth counting digits — notice how long that actually takes.

Notice

The chart is sorted biggest to smallest — the table isn't. That's not an accident. Sorting bars by size is one of the cheapest upgrades you can make to almost any chart, and it's the first thing your eye wants anyway. That gap between "read the table" and "see it instantly" is the entire reason data visualization exists.

The same chart type can be right and wrong ⚠️

A pie chart isn't a bad idea by itself. It becomes one the moment you ask it to hold too much.

✅ 4 slices — readable

Revenue by Age Group

🚩 18 slices — not readable

Revenue by Country × Product

🚩 The rule

More than 5–6 slices and a pie chart stops being readable — your eye can't rank tiny angles that are close in size. Switch to a bar chart instead: it can hold 20 categories and stay legible, because you're comparing bar length, not the angle of a wedge.

Your turn Look at the "bad" pie above. Which slice is bigger — the 3rd biggest or the 4th biggest? If you can't tell without hovering, that's the whole problem, right there.

Four questions your boss would actually ask 💼

No pivot fields, no chart type, no click-by-click steps this time. Just the question and the process. You pick the pivot, the chart, and the story — using what you learned in the last two levels.

Definition of done — every chart, every time
🏷️A real chart title, not "Chart title"
📏Both axes labeled, if the chart has axes
🔑A legend that's actually readable
💲Dollar values formatted as currency, not raw numbers
🗣️One sentence you can say out loud that answers the question
Free sanity check

Tasks 1–3 are all Sum of Revenue, so their Grand Totals should land on the exact same number — $95,176,318. Task 4 is built on Sum of Profit instead, so its Grand Total should be $42,126,410, not the revenue number — landing on $95,176,318 there means you pivoted the wrong field. If any total doesn't match its expected number, you filtered something out, picked COUNT instead of SUM, or pointed a pivot at the wrong range.

That's today 🎉

Pick the chart that fits the question, not the one that looks fanciest — build the pivot table before you ever open the chart menu — and know where Sheets and Excel quietly do things differently.

📊Bar/column = compare categories
📈Line = change over time
🥧Pie = a few shares of one whole
🔬Scatter = do two numbers move together
Quick recall

Revenue grew from $10.3M in 2017 to $29.7M in 2021 — but 2019 actually dipped in both revenue and profit before rebounding. The United States and Australia alone are almost 60% of all revenue. Adults (35–64) are about half of revenue by themselves. And a scatter chart is the one type a pivot table can't feed you — it needs raw order rows, not totals.
